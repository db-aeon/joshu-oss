# Research reports

>
> **Coming soon:** reports sorted into themes, as this chapter describes them, come with a later update of your box. Until then, a regular update has one part for each topic.

Your companion can research a topic for you on the web and write it up as a research report. The report is a PDF that you can read on your phone and forward to your team or a client as it is.

## Ask for a research report

Ask your companion the way you ask for anything else: by text message, email, jChat, Slack, or Telegram. Say what you want to know about, for example "research the market for electric bike subscriptions" or "research what heat pumps cost to run in a cold climate". In a chat, a text message, or a call, your companion tells you right away that it started, and a few minutes later it answers in the same conversation with a short summary and the report. In email, you get one reply, and it has the report.

Here is what a text conversation looks like:

> **You:** Research the market for electric bike subscriptions.
>
> **Mina:** On it. I'll send you the report in a few minutes.
>
> **Mina:** Subscriptions are growing fastest in cities that pay part of the fee, and repairs are the main cost for the companies. Two large providers changed their prices this year. Here's the full report: https://…

In email, Slack, and Telegram, the PDF comes as an attachment. In jChat, your companion opens the report on your desktop and gives you a link to it. A text message cannot carry a file, so your companion sends a link instead, and the link opens the PDF on your phone without a sign-in. If Slack or Telegram does not take the file, your companion sends the link there too.

## What is in a research report

A research report reads like a good news page about your topic. Your companion puts together everything it found, from news and the web to research papers, company filings, and social posts, and sorts it into themes. Each research report has the same parts:

- A summary at the top, in one paragraph, that ties the themes together.
- One or more themes on the background of your topic, for example what a company does and who it competes with.
- One theme for each thing that happened in the last 30 days and got coverage, for example a funding round, a lawsuit, or a new product.
- At the end, the list of the pages and posts that your companion read.

Each theme has a headline and one to three paragraphs that explain what happened. Under the paragraphs, you see the article that covers the theme best, and then the other articles, papers, and posts about it. A theme has a picture when one of its sources has a suitable one. Social posts, for example a Reddit thread or a YouTube video, are in the theme that they are about.

Your companion reads the best pages in full, and each number in a theme comes from a page or a post that the theme links to. In the news themes, your companion leaves out product pages, vendor guides, and lists of top products, because they don't report anything that happened. Your companion reads only public sources, so private groups, paid sites, and pages that need a sign-in are not in the report. If a source could not be reached, the report says so, and the report covers the other sources.

When your companion finds little about a topic, the report has fewer themes and says so.

By email, you get the summary and the first paragraph of each theme in the email itself, and the whole report as a PDF attachment.

## Choose the sections

Your companion picks the themes of a research report from what it finds. You can name the sections yourself, and the report then has those sections in your order, for example "research CLARA Analytics, with sections on funding, product launches, and competitors". You can name up to 6. Your companion sorts what it finds into your sections, and when it finds nothing for a section, the report leaves that section out.

When a name belongs to more than one company or product, tell your companion which one you mean, for example "CLARA Analytics, the claims AI company, not the Clara agent of Hippo". Your companion asks you when it sees that a topic can mean more than one thing. It keeps facts about the other company or product out of the report, also when a source mixes the two up. For a regular update, put the same words in the notes.

## Find an old report

Your companion keeps each report in the folder `Research` in **joshu's files**, as a PDF and as a page that you can open in the browser. To find an old report, ask your companion, for example "send me the electric bike report again", or open the folder yourself.

## Get regular updates on a topic

Your companion can follow a topic for you and send you an update on a schedule, with only what is new since the last update. Ask by email, text message, Slack, or Telegram. Say what to follow, how often, and anything about how the update should look. You can name more than one topic in one request, and your companion follows them all in one update.

Here is what a text conversation looks like:

> **You:** Every Friday at 8 am, email me what's new with home battery makers and the rules for home batteries. Keep it to 5 themes.
>
> **Mina:** Done. Every Friday at 8:00 am I'll email you an update on home battery makers and home battery rules. I'll look at web and news pages, research papers, and Reddit, because that's where these topics get the most coverage. I'll keep it to 5 themes. Your first update comes this Friday at 8:00 am.

Your companion sends each update by email, unless you ask for another channel, for example a text message, Slack, or Telegram. To change anything later, tell your companion, for example "add battery recycling" or "send it on Mondays only", or change it in the Research app.

## What an update looks like

An update looks like a research report without the background. Your companion puts together everything it found for all your topics since the last update, and sorts it into themes. A theme brings together the articles, papers, and posts about the same thing from all your sources, also when they came from different topics of your update. For example, a news article on a new state rebate and a Reddit thread about the same rebate are one theme.

Here is the start of an update by email:

> **Home batteries: November 6**
>
> Two battery makers raised money this week, and a second state now pays a rebate for home batteries that share power with the grid.
>
> **Hollis Energy and Brightcell raise new money**
>
> Hollis Energy raised money from two utilities to build a factory in Ohio, and it says the factory starts making batteries next year. Brightcell closed a smaller round a few days later, and it will use the money to sell its batteries through installers. Both companies say demand comes from homeowners who already have solar panels.
>
> Energy Daily: Hollis Energy raises money for Ohio battery factory
> More: Reuters, Brightcell press release, a Reddit thread in r/solar

Your companion compares what it found with the earlier updates. An update has only themes with something new. When a theme continues something from an earlier update, its paragraphs say what changed since then. Something that you already got comes back only when there is news about it, and you can see the rest in the Research app.

Your companion writes each update the way you asked: how many themes, which themes come first, or a line on why each theme matters to you. A limit such as "keep it to 5" counts themes. You can change that any time, for example "start with the rules" or "skip the why it matters lines".

By email, you get the whole update in the email itself, and the same update as a PDF attachment, so you can read it on your phone or forward it as it is. The pictures are only in the PDF, so that your email app shows the whole update. By text message, Slack, or Telegram, you get the summary and one line for each theme with its headline and a link. Ask your companion for the full update there, and it sends you the PDF.

In a regular update, your companion leaves out pages that have no date, because it can't tell whether they are new. A quiet week gives you fewer themes, and a week with nothing new sends nothing.

The themes of an update can mix your topics, so an update no longer has one part for each topic. If you want a topic on its own, set it up as its own regular update.

Your first update is a starting update. There is no earlier update to compare with, so it shows the main themes of the last week.

## Choose the sources

When you set up a regular update, your companion picks the sources that fit your topics and tells you which ones it picked and why. You can leave it like that, or pick the sources yourself, for example "only news and Reddit" or "add TikTok". You can choose from:

- Web and news
- Research papers
- Company data (the stock price and the filings of one public company)
- Reddit, X, YouTube, TikTok, and Instagram

Your companion keeps the same sources from one update to the next, so the updates stay comparable. It picks the sources again only when you change the topics.

Web and news, research papers, and company data are free for you. Posts from Reddit, X, YouTube, TikTok, and Instagram, and other sources such as the odds of prediction markets, come through the Joshu content router, and each search takes a small amount, often less than one cent, from your usage wallet. When your usage wallet is empty, your reports still come, with web and news only, and the list of sources at the end tells you that the social sources need money in the wallet.

You can also tell your companion which source to use for a kind of question, for example "use Polymarket for election odds". Your companion remembers it and uses it in your next reports.

On your own box, the social sources need a router API key. Create one in your account on the Joshu website and save it in Connectors, section **AI providers**. Without the key, your reports have web and news only.

## Ask again about an earlier report

When you ask about a topic that your companion already researched for you, it sends you what changed since that report, not a new report from the start. If it is not sure which report you mean, it asks, for example "Do you want an update of your October 21 report on Clara Analytics, or a new report?" To get a new report from the start, say so.

## The Research app

Open **Research** on your desktop to see all your reports. The app lists your regular updates, with their schedule and what the last run found, and your one-time reports, with their date. Click a report to open it, or click a regular update to see each update that your companion sent and the stories that it is following.

To set up a regular update in the app, click **New report**. Type the topics, choose **Once** or **Recurring** and the days and time, choose **Auto** or the sources you want, choose where to send it, and write any notes on how the update should look. For a report that you get once, you can also type the sections you want, one on each line, and notes on what the topic means. Then click **Create**. You can also pause, change, or delete a regular update in the app.

## Good to know

- If your topic can mean more than one thing, your companion asks you one short question before it starts. For example, "Apple" can be the company or the fruit.
- Anybody who has the link to a report can open the report, so forward the link only to people who may read the report.
- If your companion cannot finish a report within about 10 minutes, it tells you so, and you can ask again.
- If you ask on a phone call, your companion calls you back, tells you what the report found, and texts you the link. When it cannot call you, for example late in the evening or when you miss the call, it texts you the summary and the link instead.
- When there is nothing new, your companion sends you nothing. If you'd rather hear "nothing new today", tell your companion. When a regular update has had nothing new for several runs in a row, the Research app marks it as quiet, so you can change the topics or delete it.
- If your companion cannot finish an update, it sends you a short note that the update failed. The next update covers everything since the last update that you got.
- Updates come by email, text message, Slack, or Telegram. jChat cannot receive a scheduled message, so your companion cannot send updates there.
- Your companion uses your time zone from **Welcome** for the schedule.
- The last30days app is gone. If you watched topics in it, your companion moved them into one regular update with the same schedule, and its first update after the change is a starting update. Your old last30days results stay in the folder `research/last30days` in **joshu's files**.
- Each update stays in the folder `Research` in **joshu's files**, so you can ask your companion about it later, for example "what happened with the Army humanoid contract last month?"
